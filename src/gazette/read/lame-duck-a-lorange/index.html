Lame Duck à L’Orange

Doomberg◆ 原文出处 ↗ 2026-11-15 · 565 words

“A tax loophole is something that benefits the other guy. If it benefits you, it is tax reform.” – Russell B. Long

For all the attention given to the lurid, spy-versus-spy details of Jensen Huang’s targeted removal from the top post at the technology giant he founded, a rather more pedestrian threat to America’s AI dominance has long been obvious in open sources: China can build stuff much faster and cheaper than any major Western nation, including electricity-hungry data centers and the power plants that feed them. While the communist nation may be late to the data center construction game, if recent reporting is any indication, it won’t be long before it accelerates into the global pole position:

“China is building data centres at rapid speed across its energy-rich hinterland, as Beijing seeks to turn abundant electricity and cheap land into an advantage in the global race to develop AI.

The push is creating vast new computing hubs such as Ulanqab in Inner Mongolia, where 89 data centres are built or planned. The AI infrastructure build-out is among the fastest and biggest anywhere in the world…

China already has 24 gigawatts of operational data centre computing capacity, more than the rest of Asia combined but less than half the 56GW in the US, according to SemiAnalysis, the chip-focused research firm. A further 50GW is under construction or has been announced.”

China’s momentum advantage over the US in this regard looks insurmountable when one considers the absolute drubbing data center developers were handed by voters in the midterm elections held on November 3rd. In a rout with few parallels in American history, initiatives opposing the construction of the spooky boxes passed handily, while those supporting them were summarily rejected:

“Data center opponents emerged among the biggest beneficiaries of Democrats’ sweeping victories in the November midterm elections, as voters across 10 states backed restrictions or rejected proposals to develop data centers in their communities, Reuters reported last week. According to Redbud Consulting tracking, 40 of the 41 data center-related ballot measures sought to restrict their establishment.

In a majority of counties where these measures appeared on ballots, voters also shifted toward Democratic candidates in Senate races compared with the 2024 elections. The results could signal growing political pressure on the technology industry, with a Democrat-led Congress potentially pursuing tighter oversight of data center development.”

If voter preferences mattered all that much to US political leaders, the country would not find itself in an empire-ending stalemate with Iran, nor would talk of a possible restart of that war be taken seriously. Based on recent legislative maneuvering in Congress, so too might it be with the nation’s aversion to data centers. During the upcoming lame-duck session, a bill that would vastly overhaul America’s all-constraining energy permitting system stands its best chance of becoming law.

If it does, not only would a globally relevant boom in new energy development of all forms—oil, gas, wind, solar, and nuclear—break out in the US, but also many left-for-dead data center projects would suddenly become viable. If the pieces fall just right, the Jensen affair will be, at best, the second-most impactful event to rock the AI space in the closing weeks of 2026. Let’s head to Washington to judge the prospects of this transformative legislation becoming law.
