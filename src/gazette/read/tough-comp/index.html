Tough Comp

Doomberg◆ 原文出处 ↗ 2026-10-27 · 596 words

“Measure what is measurable, and make measurable what is not so.” – Galileo Galilei

Roughly two and a half years ago, we published “Mapping the War,” a guide to following the daily ebbs and flows of the war between NATO and Russia over Ukraine. In it, we listed several accessible open-source social media accounts that, despite their known biases, provided good signal in a sea of propagandistic noise.

One account profiled was the Military Summary channel on YouTube, an excellent source of real-time information that has collectively garnered more than 400 million views worldwide. By consuming the channel’s twice-daily, bite-sized summaries of the war, we became aware of several important developments long before they made headlines in the Financial Times or Washington Post. Among them were Ukraine’s huge assault on Russian cargo and tanker ships in the Sea of Azov in July, Russia’s retaliation by blockading Ukraine’s Black Sea ports and destroying Odesa’s port infrastructure, and countless other escalations.

One thing that stands out after watching hundreds of such episodes over the years is just how tough it is to inflict a knockout blow from the air using only long-range, standoff munitions. Consider Russia’s years-long campaign against Ukraine’s energy infrastructure. Russia has fired thousands of highly advanced ballistic missiles and tens of thousands of cutting-edge drones in a seemingly relentless daily campaign, but only now, with Kyiv effectively out of air-defense missiles, is the target deteriorating at a truly alarming rate.

If you live in Britain, you have probably been told that this pace exists because Russia is a paper tiger whose missiles don’t shoot straight, or Russian President Vladimir Putin has cancer (did you see his hands twitch?), or his agents are too busy Q-Tipping your local politician’s bidet to focus on winning the war. In the rest of the world, the natural conclusion is that it is prohibitively difficult to inflict sufficient damage from afar to force capitulation, especially against a country as large and as clearly motivated to fight as Ukraine.

Against such a backdrop, breaking news published in the Wall Street Journal over the weekend piqued our interest:

“American warships in the Middle East are preparing for an escalation in fighting in case President Trump opts for a fresh round of attacks against Iran in the coming weeks, according to US officials familiar with the planning.

During a rare visit to a US aircraft carrier and guided-missile destroyer operating in the Arabian Sea, The Wall Street Journal observed flight operations, as well as a significant replenishment of munitions and supplies—including a large delivery of standoff land attack missiles and bunker-buster bombs designed to penetrate ground targets.”

Given Iran’s credible threats to unleash an extraordinarily violent response to any renewed strikes on its territory, just what, exactly, are US President Donald Trump and his advisers hoping to accomplish with yet another round of fighting? Is a civilization-ending knockout blow to Iran even remotely possible using a couple of aircraft carriers, some long-range bombers taking off stateside, and what remains of American air bases in the Middle East? Can we learn anything from how challenging it has been for Russia to deal such a blow to Ukraine, despite sharing a border more than 1,000 miles long and enjoying access to its entire formidable military-industrial complex for the task?

A direct comparison between Iran and Ukraine is not only readily available using open sources, but the numbers will come as a sobering shock. Let’s have a look.
